# Worked examples

These are illustrative analyses written for this draft, not independent model-test results. They use the ACID Method by Design On Acid.

## Workshop invitation

**Content:** “A free poster workshop for beginners. Saturday, 14:00. Bring one photo. Book a place.”

**Context:** Assume this is a public invitation for local first-time participants; no venue or booking link is supplied.

**Overall:** The offer is accessible and concrete, but a prospective participant cannot yet complete the journey from interest to attendance.

| Lens | Evidence and interpretation | Useful change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Abstraction | “Free”, “for beginners”, and “bring one photo” make cost, audience, and preparation clear. “Saturday” leaves the date uncertain. | Add the calendar date, venue, and duration. |
| C — Creativity | Bringing a personal photo gives the activity a participatory starting point. The invitation does not yet convey the expressive possibilities of making a poster. This excerpt cannot establish originality against other workshops. | Show one example of how a photo becomes a poster, or describe a specific creative possibility. |
| I — Intention | “Book a place” gives an explicit action, but no booking destination is present in the supplied text. | Attach a booking link and clarify any capacity limit if one exists. |
| D — Depth | “For beginners” may reduce anxiety for newcomers; this is a hypothesis about reception. The text does not say what participants can expect to experience together. | Add a truthful detail about support during the workshop, then ask beginners whether the invitation feels welcoming. |

**Synthesis:** Preserve the clear preparation instruction and beginner positioning. Add practical attendance and booking details first; then use a concrete creative example to connect expression with personal relevance. Ask a new reader to find when and where it happens and complete a test booking. Do not invent the missing details in a rewrite.

## Comparing two headlines

**Content A:** “Make a poster from your own photo. Free beginner workshop. Book your place.”

**Content B:** “Turn a memory into something the street can see. Join our poster workshop.”

**Context:** Assume a landing-page headline targeting beginners. The goal is workshop bookings. No conversion data is available.

| Lens | A | B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abstraction | Names the output, personal input, price, and level. | “Memory” is suggestive but leaves format and level less explicit. |
| Creativity | Direct expression with a concrete transformation. | Reframes poster-making as moving a private memory into public view. |
| Intention | Gives a clear booking action aligned with the assumed goal. | Invites participation, with less clarity on what happens next. |
| Depth | The personal photo offers a route to connection. | The memory/public expression relationship may evoke personal meaning; audience response remains untested. |

**Synthesis:** A better supports immediate understanding and action in this assumed context. B offers an expressive direction worth testing. Consider B as the headline with A's practical detail as supporting copy, provided “the street can see” reflects what actually happens. Test comprehension and booking behaviour with intended participants; the framework alone cannot predict a conversion winner.
